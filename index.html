Alberto Maria Mongardini

Measuring the economics of online deception — and turning deception itself into a defensive instrument.

Online fraud is not chaos; it is a market. My research measures that market at scale — how meme coins simulate growth and extract profit, how conspiracy communities monetize their audiences, how fake channels and bots operate across Telegram and blockchains — and studies how defenders can answer with deception of their own: honeypots, decoys, and accountability mechanisms. I am an Assistant Professor (tenure track) at DTU Compute, with a PhD in Computer Science from Sapienza University of Rome (2024, summa cum laude).

Research

The economics of online harm

Much of my work measures how deception makes money. I build large datasets and detection methods that expose the market structure behind online fraud: how meme coins simulate growth and extract profit from investors, how conspiracy communities monetize their audiences, and how fake channels, wash traders, and sniper bots operate at scale.

Selected workA Midsummer Meme's Dream (USENIX Security '26) · The Conspiracy Money Machine (USENIX Security '25) · TGDataset (KDD '25) · fake/clone channel detection (ACM TWEB, IEEE ICWS '23) · NFT wash trading (IEEE ICDCS '23) · sniper bots (ACM TOIT, CAAW '23)

Deception as defense

If attackers rely on deception to extract value, defenders can use it to observe, mislead, and hold them accountable. This line studies honeypots, decoy assets, and accountability mechanisms: deceptive defenses against intellectual-property theft, IP camera honeypots, LLM-generated honeywords, and attribute-driven accountability for Bitcoin transactions.

Selected workDARD (IEEE T-IFS) · Stealth and Beyond (ACNS '26) · ongoing work on honeypots and AI-driven deception with E. Vasilomanolakis (DTU)

Datasets & Software

  • TGDataset

    GitHub · Zenodo

    The largest public dataset of Telegram channels (120k+ channels, 400M+ messages), built to enable systematic research on deception, misinformation, and fraud. Includes preprocessing scripts and schema documentation.

    Used in KDD '25, USENIX Security '25, IEEE ICWS '23, and ACM TWEB.

  • Meme Coin Market Manipulations

    Zenodo

    Replication package for the cross-chain analysis of 34,988 meme coins (Ethereum, BSC, Solana, Base): the full longitudinal dataset, Dune Analytics SQL queries, and Python detection logic for wash trading, liquidity-pool price inflation, rug pulls, and pump-and-dumps.

    Official artifact of USENIX Security '26.

  • Conspiracy Dataset on Telegram

    GitHub

    A curated list of channels involved in conspiracy narratives and monetization schemes, categorized by conspiracy type with subscriber and message-frequency metadata.

    Supports USENIX Security '25.

  • Anti-Sniping Detection Regex

    Zenodo

    A curated collection of regular expressions for detecting smart-contract features that counter sniper bots: cooldowns, transfer limits, and blacklists.

    Used in CAAW '23 and ACM TOIT.
